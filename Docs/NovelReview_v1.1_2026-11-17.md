# Review of version 1.1: *The Road That Remembers*

Reviewed 2026-11-17 from a copy of `Docs/The Road That Remembers v1.1.docx` (about 14,600 words: Prologue, 31 chapters, Epilogue). I read the whole book cold first, before opening the first review or the change log, then compared key passages with a copy of the original. Neither .docx was changed.

Locations are given as a chapter plus a short quote, so you can find each one with Word's search.

---

## Verdict

**v1.1 is clearly better than the original, and it is close to "good enough", but it is not there yet.** The original had around eighteen places where a careful reader would stop and lose trust. Bjarne changed sex, people were in two places at once, the ember was in two hands, and game words like "Life Orbs" and "extraction light" were in the prose. Nearly all of these are fixed, and fixed neatly. The new scenes (Sigrid and Liv's rescue, the nameless traveller's thread, Bread staying to keep the fire) are in the book's voice and make it better.

What is left is concentrated in Acts II and III: what happened to Sigrun, where the group is going after they meet the Waker, a leftover line in Chapter 26, and the mountain troll who disappears. All of them can be fixed with about ten small edits, most of them one sentence, and none needs a new scene. After those edits the book meets your goal: a good, consistent story that a reader can follow without getting confused.

---

## What works

- **The opening hook and its payoff.** Liv underlines "Granheim" twice in the prologue ("If I ask again, don't answer quickly"), and the others forget the bell as soon as they hear it. In Chapter 12, Liv is found still reading the worn word aloud so she won't lose it. It is the best new scene in v1.1.
- **The nameless traveller.** "Sigrid introduced them by a name Mara could not recall a minute later" turns a loose end into the book's quietest horror. The count of eight against "I hear seven", the uncut eighth line on the post, and the last mention among the missing are all clean.
- **Act I is now easy to follow.** A reader can count who comes home: Ingrid, Bjarne, Tor, Runa, Eirik, the four from other winters, then Sigrid and Liv. The marker post makes the count visible.
- **Testing a false voice is a strong, repeatable idea.** The unmarked knuckle (Ch 17), "old bear" (Ch 20, 22, 27) and "none of her impatience" (Ch 24) each test the voice with a private detail, so the reader learns a rule without being told it.
- **The bell rule now explains the bells after the fact.** "A bell rings when someone, somewhere, says a lost name aloud" (Ch 26) quietly explains the prologue (Bjarne says "Granheim"), Chapter 1 (the traveller has just been named and forgotten) and Chapter 14 (Eirik calling out names).
- **Plain prose and dry humour.** For example: "Then we ran out of bridge." / "That's what it always means." / "Is there another kind?"
- **The ending is earned.** The Waker does not get his family back, Bread keeps the fire, and the epilogue's bell opens the next story without cheating this one.

## Remaining issues, ranked by how much they hurt a reader

### 1. Sigrun: who is she, and is she lost? (Ch 3, 4, 15, 21, 31) — *high*

- **Ch 3:** "When I left home, I knew the sound of her steps on the stair. This morning I had to think to remember her name."
- **Ch 4:** "…though Sigrun was not missing."
- **Ch 15:** "She read Sigrun last, though they had not found her yet."
- **Ch 21:** "Beside it lay a red thread caught on a splinter of wood." / "We need to know whether Sigrun is alive."
- **Ch 31:** "I told you to sew the thread in properly."

**Problem.** In Ch 3, Sigrid has just been talking about Liv, so "her" reads as Liv. The reader does not learn that Sigrun is Eirik's sister until Chapter 12. (The original said "my sister"; the v1.1 edit removed it, see New problems.) After that, the book says Sigrun is not missing (Ch 4), then not yet found (Ch 15). In Ch 21 her thread lies at a snowy camp with no explanation, and in Ch 31 she comes home through the stones. A reader cannot tell whether she was safe at home, lost on the road, or lost in time. Eirik's thread is the book's main emotional subplot, and the reunion in Chapter 31 depends on it.

Also, Sigrun is the one who stitched the thread, so "I told you to sew the thread in properly" has the joke the wrong way round.

**Suggested minimal edit** (this matches the story notes, where Sigrun is one of the Lost):

- **Ch 3:** "When I left home, I knew the sound of my sister's steps on the stair. She went up this road years ago and never came back. This morning I had to think to remember her name."
  - This also gives Sigrid's sharp look a reason, and it makes her Ch 27 confession hit harder.
- **Ch 4:** change "though Sigrun was not missing" to "though Sigrun was not one of the four."
- **Ch 21:** after "afraid to touch", add: "'Hers,' he said. 'She was here.'"
- **Ch 31:** "I told you you'd need that thread."

*Alternative:* if you meant Sigrun was safe at home and got lost later while looking for Eirik, keep Ch 4. Instead:

- **Ch 15:** "…though, as far as anyone knew, Sigrun was safe at home."
- **Ch 21:** "'Hers. She came after me.'"

Either version works. The book just needs to pick one.

### 2. After they meet the Waker, the reader doesn't know where the group is going (Ch 19–25) — *high*

- **Ch 19:** "The Portal had marked their map with a line of pale ash, leading north through a pass called the Wolf's Jaw."
- **Ch 23** (the first time the place is named): "The debt comes due at the First Hearth."

**Problem.** Act II starts with a clear goal: find the Waker. They find him in Chapter 20. After that, the group keeps travelling for eight chapters with no destination anyone has stated. The First Hearth is first named by the Jaw-keeper as if the reader already knows it, and by Chapter 25 it is "the route to the First Hearth". A reader can follow the scenes one by one but loses the reason for them, which is the most common way to lose a reader in the middle of a book.

**Suggested minimal edit.** In Ch 19, after "Because the other roads remember what waits at their ends", add:

> "Where is the Waker going?" Mara asked.
> "To the First Hearth, where the pact was made and every door begins. If he opens it alone, nothing will close it again."

From then on, every later mention of the First Hearth pays off this line.

### 3. A leftover line in Chapter 26: Sigrid asks about something she was there for (Ch 26) — *medium-high*

- "Sigrid looked at the Heartroot cutting. 'Did you find out what it wants?'"

**Problem.** In the original, Sigrid was missing until Chapter 26, so the question made sense. In v1.1 she was rescued in Chapter 12. She fought the Heartroot, took the watch, and called the names when the black door opened. This is the one visible continuity break the revision left behind, and it comes just before the climax.

Chapter 26 also repeats the Chapter 12 rescue almost exactly: Sigrid hurt beside Liv, a dry greeting, "Took you long enough." Liv doesn't answer when called ("Answer me"), but she is fine in the next chapter, and the book never says why.

**Suggested minimal edit:**

- "Sigrid looked back at the folded trees. 'Did you find out what this place wants?'" The rest of the exchange ("To be left in peace." / "Good. We have that in common.") stays as it is.
- *Optional:* to make the scene differ from Chapter 12, let Liv answer when they find her: "Liv opened one eye. 'I was answering. You weren't listening.'"

### 4. The mountain troll is set up as hunting the Waker, then dropped (Ch 15, 23) — *medium*

- **Ch 15:** "'Walking north.' 'After what?' 'The Waker.'"
- **Ch 15:** "…shut a man's wife and daughter on the other side."
- **Ch 23:** "walking north ahead of them. It did not look down."

**Problem.** The troll starts the plot by breaking the ring in Chapter 6. It mourns at the Heartroot and is said to be after the Waker. In v1.1 it is seen once on a ridge in Chapter 23, ignoring the Waker who is walking right below it, and then never again. The Chapter 15 line about "a man's wife and daughter" is never tied to the Waker, so the hint stays hidden.

**Suggested minimal edit (two sentences):**

- **Ch 29**, after "He had run when the first villagers forgot their names", add: "Behind him, the mountain troll came down and broke the door, with Elian and Nessa still on the other side."
- **Ch 31**, in the Waker's paragraph, add: "Some evenings the mountain troll stood on the ridge above the stones and watched him work. It never came closer."

### 5. The same explanations come round three or four times (Ch 14–30) — *medium*

- **The pact failing.** Explained in the Ch 14 vision, again in the Portal's summary in Ch 15, and again in Ch 18: "It had tried to hold the old passage closed while the pact failed around it."
- **"It can copy the sound but not the person."** Said four times:
  - Ch 9: "can imitate a voice. It cannot remember why someone chose to speak"
  - Ch 22: "The Hunger could copy the sound. It could not make the hand belong to anyone."
  - Ch 27: "The Hunger can copy the sound. It can't take what you keep giving each other."
  - Ch 30: "The words were exact. The warmth behind them was not."
- **The Waker's past.** Told in pieces in Ch 15, 20, 22, 24 and 29.
- **A glimpse of loved ones through a door.** It happens five times: Ch 17, 20, 21, 25 and 27, before the seventh door in Ch 28.

**Problem.** Each repeat is fine on its own, but together they slow Acts II and III and make scenes feel alike. A reader who already understands starts to skim.

**Suggested minimal edit:**

- Cut the Ch 18 sentences "The Heartroot branch glowed faintly. Mara understood why its roots had choked the way. It had not meant to attack them. It had tried to hold the old passage closed while the pact failed around it." Chapters 14 and 15 have already said this.
- Cut the Ch 22 sentences "The Hunger could copy the sound. It could not make the hand belong to anyone." Keep "A name was not a lock. It was a hand held out in the dark."
- *If you want more:* the face in the pool in Ch 27 is the weakest of the door glimpses. The chapter would still work as Sigrid's confession plus the stories that drain the pool.

### 6. The rule for spotting the false voice is garbled where it is stated (Ch 22) — *medium*

- "Because it answered to my name. My daughter never did."

**Problem.** The voice didn't *answer to* his name; it *called him by* it. This is the line that explains the rule the climax relies on, so it needs to be exact.

**Suggested minimal edit:** "Because it called me by my name. My daughter never did."

### 7. The Waker's village is never named, and "we find that village, too" is dropped (Ch 24, 29, 31) — *medium-low*

- **Ch 24:** "I left a village behind me with its names half-eaten." / "Then we find that village, too."
- **Ch 31:** "The Waker stayed at Granheim."

**Problem.** The ending suggests Granheim is the Waker's village: he stays there and helps "rebuild the houses nearest the standing stones". That would tie the prologue mystery (travellers forgetting the road, the village whose name slips away) to the Waker. It is never said, so Sigrid's promise seems to be forgotten.

**Suggested minimal edit (if that is what you meant):** in Ch 31, write "The Waker stayed at Granheim, the village he had run from." This one clause pays off the prologue, Liv's rubbing and Sigrid's promise.

### 8. The blue door's "consent" rule and the walk to it (Ch 25–26) — *medium-low*

- **Ch 25:** "…without the Waker's consent: it was his door, and he had opened it first."

**Problem.** "He had opened it first" is confusing, because he has only just opened it, in this scene. The rule reads as invented to fit. The movement is also muddled: they leave the room that has the blue door, go back up to the surface, and then walk toward the blue door.

**Suggested minimal edit:**

- "…it was his door, and it would open only for him."
- Optional: add a clause after "as the passage shifted", such as "the room was gone, and the door with it, somewhere deeper under the valley." This explains why they have to walk.

### 9. The clearing is now "at the edge of the meadow" (Ch 31) — *low-medium*

- "Mara returned to the clearing, the old stone ring at the edge of the meadow."

**Problem.** The party walked from the meadow, slept in a cave and walked on to reach the ring. Putting the clearing at the meadow's edge invites the question "why didn't they just walk home in Act I?"

**Suggested minimal edit:** "Mara returned to the clearing. Now that the road had stopped lengthening, it was a short walk from the meadow, as Sigrid had always said." This calls back to the prologue's "A short walk, Sigrid had said."

### 10. Three small slips on the first pages (Prologue, Ch 1, Ch 2) — *low, but this is where readers decide whether to trust the book*

- **Prologue:** "Ingrid held the lantern closer. 'Is it a map?' Tor asked. 'It is a warning,' she said."
  - Now "she" could be Ingrid. Use "Liv said."
- **Ch 1:** "That's not one of the reasons I offered."
  - Sigrid offered no reasons, so this is unclear. Use "That wasn't in the contract."
- **Ch 2:** "The sun already stood at noon." is followed two lines later by "The sun had gone behind the trees."
  - The two sun lines read as contradicting each other. Change the second to "Then the sun went out."

### 11. A recurring sentence habit: a cryptic answer followed by the narrator's verdict — *low*

- **Ch 5:** "The answer annoyed Mara because it sounded true without explaining anything."
- **Ch 8:** "There was no comfort in the answer. Mara preferred that to a lie."
- **Ch 15:** "The answer was irritating enough to sound honest."
- **Ch 20:** "It was no apology, but it was honest."
- **Ch 22:** "For once, his answer sounded like a beginning."

**Problem.** One of these is character. Five of them become a tic.

**Suggested minimal edit:** cut the Ch 15 and Ch 20 lines. The dialogue already carries them.

### 12. Small items (fix if you're in there anyway)

- **Ch 20:** "People keep saying that as though I stole it." No one has said "living flame" before. Use "You say that as though I stole it."
- **Ch 20:** The Waker's "I saw the light come down" repeats Ingrid's line in Ch 8 word for word, and he is far to the north. Use "The whole north saw the light come down."
- **Ch 24:** "as the Portal had done in the forest". The Portal drew the ash line on their map (Ch 19). Use "as the Portal had done on their map".
- **Ch 23:** There are two valleys (the snow valley in Ch 20–22, then the Troll Valley), and readers may merge them. Use "Beyond the snow valley, the Troll Valley began at a wall of ice."
- **Ch 26:** "The valley had taken years off Gunnar" sounds as if he got younger. Use "The valley had taken more than time from Gunnar."
- **Ch 30:** "I have another." The meaning is unclear. To make it land, add: "He nodded at the people around the basin."
- **Ch 31:** "Mara took up her lantern." It went out on the stone in Ch 30. Use "Mara lit her lantern from the hearth and took it up."
- **Loose ends nobody will miss much:**
  - Runa's severed root (Ch 5).
  - The blue flames "in the woods" that Ingrid warns about (Ch 9).
  - The cairn man (Ch 19).
  - Eirik's flat "No" to the bell in Ch 1.

  None of these needs fixing. They are worth knowing about if you want hooks for the game.

**Not problems, so leave them as they are.** These are deliberate open threads, and they suit a story behind a game:

- Mara's mother is never found or named.
- The traveller is never found.
- The Waker's family is never recovered.
- The four from other winters stay sketches.

## New problems the revision introduced

1. **Ch 3, "When I left home, I knew the sound of her steps":** the original said "When my sister left home". The I20 fix rightly made Eirik the one who left, but it dropped "my sister", so "her" now points at Liv. Sigrun goes unexplained for nine chapters. *Fix:* "my sister's steps" (part of issue 1).
2. **Ch 26, "Did you find out what it wants?":** a line left over after Sigrid's rescue moved to Ch 12 (issue 3).
3. **Ch 26 now repeats the Ch 12 rescue.** Both have Sigrid hurt beside Liv and a dry greeting. Liv's silence is unexplained (issue 3).
4. **Prologue, "Ingrid held the lantern closer":** this makes the next "she said" ambiguous (issue 10).
5. **Ch 2, "The sun already stood at noon":** the new sentence now sits right before "The sun had gone behind the trees" (issue 10).
6. **"I saw the light come down" is now said word for word by both Ingrid (Ch 8) and the Waker (Ch 20)** (item 12).
7. **Ch 31, "the old stone ring at the edge of the meadow":** saying this outright makes the long walk in Act I look odd (issue 9).
8. **Ch 23's troll sighting brings the "after the Waker" thread back without closing it.** The troll ignores the man it is meant to be hunting (issue 4).
9. **Ch 25, "he had opened it first":** the added reason is confusing (issue 8).
10. **Ch 12 has doubled in length (about 780 words, against an average of about 430).** It now covers two returns, four strangers, the Portal's history and a rescue scene. It reads fine, but the four strangers blur together. *No change needed;* just don't add more to it.

**No tone shifts were introduced.** The new passages match the book's plain, dry voice. The added rule sentences (Ch 13: "The woods nearest the clearing could be walked…"; Ch 9: "Mine I lit myself…") are short enough not to drag.

## The short list to "good enough"

These ten edits, all one or two sentences, should take about an hour. Everything else in this report is optional.

1. **Ch 3:** "my sister's steps on the stair. She went up this road years ago and never came back." Then **Ch 4:** "not one of the four". **Ch 21:** "'Hers,' he said. 'She was here.'" **Ch 31:** "I told you you'd need that thread." *(Sigrun, issue 1)*
2. **Ch 19:** add the Portal's line naming the First Hearth as where the Waker is going, and why it matters. *(issue 2)*
3. **Ch 26:** "Sigrid looked back at the folded trees. 'Did you find out what this place wants?'" *(issue 3)*
4. **Ch 29:** add the troll breaking the door with Elian and Nessa behind it. **Ch 31:** add the troll watching the Waker from the ridge. *(issue 4)*
5. **Ch 22:** "Because it called me by my name." *(issue 6)*
6. **Ch 18:** cut the four sentences that re-explain the Heartroot. **Ch 22:** cut "The Hunger could copy the sound…". *(issue 5)*
7. **Ch 31:** "The Waker stayed at Granheim, the village he had run from." (if intended) *(issue 7)*
8. **Ch 25:** "it was his door, and it would open only for him." *(issue 8)*
9. **Prologue:** "Liv said". **Ch 1:** "That wasn't in the contract." **Ch 2:** "Then the sun went out." *(issue 10)*
10. **Ch 31:** "it was a short walk from the meadow, as Sigrid had always said." *(issue 9)*

After these, the book does what you asked for: a good, consistent story that keeps the reader engaged and doesn't confuse them with details.
