# Review: HUD, combat feel, exploring the Haven, missing systems (2026-11-19)

For your review. You asked, in order: "look for improvements on the Main hud. right now its cluttered" ("fix it"); then "send an agent
to do a combat feedback check ... implement its suggestions", "research and implement the best solutions to making Safe Haven the best
possible world in terms of exploration and design ... what do we need in order to make exploring haven fun?", "research what systems
and design may be lacking? improve those", "do not stop to ask questions ... note what you have done in a document for my review".

How it was done: three research agents (combat feel, exploring the Haven, missing systems) each read the existing reviews first and
wrote a ranked report (`Docs/CombatFeelReview_2026-11-19.md`, `Docs/HavenExplorationReview_2026-11-19.md`,
`Docs/SystemsGapReview_2026-11-19.md`); I built from them, checked each change in Play or by test, and list here what was built, what
was left and why. Nothing here has been played by hand: each part says how it was checked.

## 1. The HUD (built)

Seen in Play before and after, in the Haven, on a raid and in a fight (the comparison sheet was sent with this). A new option,
**Options › Gameplay › HUD: Full / Contextual (default) / Minimal**; Full is the HUD as it was. Every HUD piece asks one rule
(`UI/HudRules`) for what it shows when nothing is happening.

| What | Before | Contextual now |
|---|---|---|
| Health, stamina, hunger, thirst | four full bars with "100 / 100" all game (they dimmed to 40%, which still read as on) | gone while everything is full and quiet; back on any change, a fight or a low need; hunger and thirst only below 60% or just after a meal |
| The day's timeline | five labelled times across the top of the Haven, one behind the portal | the clock and weather only; the timeline from two hours before dusk and an hour and a half before dawn, and over the world map |
| Skill bar | nine slots, four empty frames | filled slots only; the empty ones while dragging or with the skills window open (every key keeps its place) |
| "Skills (N)" and the experience strip | always | while it has something to say: something to spend, experience just earned, a raid's bonus, a level |
| Tutorials | a framed box "4 unread tutorials · (T)" mid-right | an unframed book with "4 (T)", with the HUD's soft shadow |
| Minimap | a third of the screen's height | three-quarters size, the region's name on it |
| Quest list | every quest with every step, done ones in green | the quest on top with its open steps; the rest by title (Minimal: the top one alone) |
| Noise meter ("Silent") | always at home and on raids | near something hostile, or while sneaking |
| A far object's marker | its name and "Move closer to interact" in red | its name, softly |
| The same message twice | two lines | one line, "×2" |
| Two "Dodged" at once | drawn over each other | one; close numbers and words step upwards |

Two of these were bugs against their own rules, fixed first: the noise meter counted any creature in the scene ("not at a quiet home"
in its own summary), and the raid's quest list showed "Listen to the portal (E)" on every raid ("only what can be done there").

Note: this Editor has **Always Show Vitals** on in its saved options, so your bars stay lit until you switch it off; the screenshots
were taken with it off in memory only.

Tests: `HudTests` (the default, the timeline's window, a repeated message counted). The rest was checked in Play.

## 2. Combat feel (built)

From `Docs/CombatFeelReview_2026-11-19.md`; the numbers are its items. All of it is tuning in **CombatFeel**, **CombatTechnique**
and **PlayerMovement** (Resources), so a value you dislike is one field.

| # | What was wrong | What it does now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A sweep through three wolves froze the game three times in a row (up to 4 × 455 ms for the hammer art) | one stop per swing: each further body lengthens it by a share, to a ceiling (Stop Ceiling 0.25 s, Kill Stop Ceiling 0.3 s) |
| 2 | The parry glint came as the 0.15 s window opened, faster than anyone reacts (4 parries in 70 minutes of your play) | a pale **parry line** on the strike's fan where the fill stands mid-window (press as the front crosses it), and the glint plus a short "ting" 0.12 s before the window opens, on strikes at you only; a strike that may hold its wind-up never cues before its hold point, so holds still bait. The window is unchanged |
| 3 | Every monster attack had Impact 1: a rootling's grasp and a troll's stomp landed alike | 38 attacks given Impact 0.7-2.0 by weight; stomps, charges and quakes pull you a little; being hit stops and shakes by Impact |
| 4 | Knockback went by critical: a dagger's sure critical shoved a troll further than a hammer's heavy | by the blow's Impact and technique; a critical adds a little; bosses never move; a plain sword slash 0.35 m (seen: 0.33 m on a Draug) |
| 5 | After a flinch, the next blow could land before you could act | the last part of a flinch can be dodged out of, and the monsters' next turn waits for it |
| 6 | A dodge pressed a moment early (still cooling down) was lost | pressed in the last 0.2 s it is kept and happens on time; earlier, a short "no" sound |
| 7 | A parry, a counter and a perfect dodge stopped no harder than a plain hit | they stop harder; the weapon glows pale steel while a counter is ready |
| 8 | The end of a fight wasn't marked; the fight music held for 5 s of quiet | the last foe near you falling (in a fight of two foes or 4 s) slows the game briefly after its stop, a low drum and a breath of wind play, and the fight is over at once. Not for a boss (it has its own stop) or wildlife |
| 9 | Damage numbers were one white for blows dealt and taken, the same for a companion's, several per sweep | blows on you red, small, no pop; others' small and faint; yours sized by the share of health they took; blows within 0.25 s on one body count up in one number |
| 10 | Every sword, dagger, spear and staff whooshed alike; hammers sounded like hatchets; an interrupt played the block's clang | dagger and spears: a short rising thrust; hammers, club, maul: a slow low flutter; greatswords: a steel edge over the axe's air; an interrupt: a dry crack. Swords and axes keep theirs |
| 11 | A charged heavy was a blind count until the chime | the weapon's light warms from ember to white-hot as it charges and flickers at full; a rising sound under it stops when you let go |
| 12 | A creature out of frame could strike you with no warning | a chevron at the screen's edge on its side, in the strike's colour (a dark rim for a great blow), growing with its wind-up; three at most |
| 13 | The first hit on an elite wiped its coloured rings (the flash reset them) | each renderer's own colour is put back after the flash; outlines and labels aren't flashed |
| 15 | The only camera motion was random shake | your heavy, finisher and art blows push the frame along the blow (a hammer heavy 9 cm), once a swing, with less shake; a heavy blow taken pushes it the way it went |

**Not built, needs you: 14.** A last-moment dodge *away* from a blow (it misses you, so nothing is "dodged through") doesn't count
as a perfect dodge; only dodging into a blow does. The review suggests counting a near miss, with half the Focus. That changes the
parry economy you decided, so it waits for your yes.

**The sounds** are Python (`Tools/Audio/combat.py`: Parry Cue, Thrust Swing, Blunt Swing, Great Blade Swing, Interrupt Break, Charge
Rise, Fight Won). Unheard by you; if one is poor, it is one recipe to change, or a slot for a LocalSpawn take.

**Checked:** in Play against a Draug at the Haven: no cue on its lunge (a charge), its cleave and backswing cued 0.18-0.23 s before
contact (at the Editor's background frame rate), the line at 0.9 of the reach; a sword slash moved it 0.33 m; three hits in one frame
gave one 0.09 s stop. Tests: `CombatWeightTests`, `CombatReadabilityTests`; the 21 combat test classes green.

## 3. Exploring the Haven (built)

From `Docs/HavenExplorationReview_2026-11-19.md` (its numbers). The research found the Haven well built as ground and badly built as a
reason to walk: 13 of 27 named places gave nothing but the find, the minimap showed none of them, a far find paid what a near one did,
and from the top-down camera nothing past 14 m ahead is in frame. Everything here is stood **at runtime** from one data asset,
`Resources/HavenPlaceVerbs`, at the atlas's places: the scene file is untouched and a world regeneration can't wipe it (the place
props are raid set pieces too, so they carry nothing of the Haven's).

| # | What it does now |
|---|---|
| 0 | The playtest log writes where you walk at home (every 5 s) and PlaceFound, RegionEntered, LookoutUsed, PlaceFindTaken rows, so the next playtest shows where players go |
| 1 | **Six lookouts** (the Lookout, Raven and Windy Cairns, the Ruined Watchtower, the Broken Tower, the Standing Stones): E "Look out over the land" lifts the fog 110 m round, marks every place within 180 m as **seen** (grey on the world map until walked to, and only then found and paid), and the view pulls out to 1.9× and flatter while you stand there. The first look gives Insight and you name one unfound place you can see, with its bearing ("A pale scar in the hillside: Grey Quarry. To the north-east.") |
| 2 | Every kind of place says something as you come near (two thoughts each, "Look closer" for the second), remembered across loads. **The Old Quarry** has the old masons' cut stone stacked to take once: 6 Stone Blocks, the altar's cost; **the Grey Quarry** 4 Granite Blocks. Once per character (each player's own in co-op) |
| 3 | Places heard before seen: crows over the barrow and the burnt farmstead by day, the Wolf Den's howl at night, ravens at the cairns (two at most at once, none in a fight). The **minimap** now shows the Haven's found places within 150 m (none before) and a faint mark on the rim for an unfound one within 70 m |
| 4 | **"Cut Stone"**, a green Haven quest: once The Alchemist asks for the Purification Altar, the portal remembers masons in the north of Ravenwood; walk out to the Old Quarry (290 m, past the Charcoal Camp) and take the stack. Held for a homecoming like every Haven quest |
| 5a | True labels: Ravenwood (the richest wood) Dangerous, the Wolfwood Dangerous (it said Deadly), Greyfell Wary, "nothing lives up there but the wind" |
| 6a | A find pays 10 Insight + 1 per 15 m from the portal (18 in the meadows, 48 at the far edge; it was 25 flat). The world map counts a region's places ("RAVENWOOD 2/4"). The land known adds Haven renown, 1 a place, to 30 |

**Checked in Play** (no save slot loaded, so nothing reached your save): 29 place holders stood; the Raven Cairn's look gave its Insight
once and marked 3 places, a second look nothing; the cut stone taken once (0 → 6); the stack seen on open ground beside the quarry; the
farmstead's mark on the minimap 50 m off. Not yet seen in Play: the world map's grey seen-places and region counts, the place sounds
(their timing is by chance), the quest from start to end. Tests: `HavenExplorationTests`; `SaveCoverageTests` covers the seen set;
the quest audit knows the place layer's targets.

**Left** (in the research, not built): verbs for the barrow (a night door and a Draug), the Wolf Den's pack and hoard, relighting the
Burnt Farmstead's hearth, the camps' daily goods, the stones' carvings; seven new places in the empty wedges (the falls stairs among
them: their landmarks are still unnamed); "A Stone by the Water" (needs the boundary stone's reading); the found-in-the-Haven pages; a
heatmap of the walk rows. Each is the same data asset plus a component, so they can come one at a time.

## 4. Missing systems (built)

From `Docs/SystemsGapReview_2026-11-19.md` (its numbers).

| # | What it does now |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Wanted at home.** The portal's next tier is always a target, and up to three building levels can be tracked (H › Buildings › "Track"). What they cost less what is home (bag + stash) is *wanted*: a material's tooltip says "Wanted at home: 4 more for Smithy II (6/10)", picking one up on a raid says "Copper Ingot 7/12 for Smithy II", and the raid's briefing lists "Wanted at home: …" with "(likely here)" |
| 2 | **A fuller homecoming.** About 2 s after coming home from a raid, a card "While you were away": orders ready at the stations, what your people made, what is now within reach (a target everything home covers), asks you can answer. It takes no input and fades by itself; with nothing to say, the old workers' line shows as before |
| 3 | **Pack as last time** (a stash button and the action wheel): the draughts, food, bandages, arrows and quickbar items your last raid set out with, from the stash into the bag, saying what was short. Gear and materials never move. **Mend everything worn** on the repair bench |
| 4 | **A reason to stay.** The first danger stage of Misty Woods, Troll Valley and Moss Marsh reveals a cache 70-140 m away, out of sight and at least 50 m from an exit, gold on the minimap's rim: "Something glints beyond, to the north. The woods give more to those who stay." It holds +50%; the second stage's hoard (a locked barrow chest) +80% |
| 7a | **Failure that teaches.** The death report lists the last three blows that mattered: who, with what, how much, and whether it was a great blow, unblockable, from behind or from out of view. Two opt-in assists: Damage Taken › Grows With Falls (90%, 2% less per fall, to 50%) and Accessibility › Raid Length (+25% / +50%). Defaults unchanged |

**Left:** 5 companions who differ (M-L), 6 the Hearth morale view, 8 the raid's tale and the Chronicle, 9 the Works bundles, 10-12, 13
co-op (showing the gear gap needs a new invite field and a two-machine test), 14 defence with choices (after the playtest), 15 the
endgame ladder; 7b the Journey presets (the review asks you first). The Hoard's elite guard was left out.
