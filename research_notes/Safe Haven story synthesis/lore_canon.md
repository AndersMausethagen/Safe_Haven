# Lore canon, approvals, and story gaps

## What is established and how the current story flows

### Takeaway
Safe Haven’s built story is a three-part emotional promise already clear in the first act: the forest makes people forget one another; a tended hearth gives the lost a way home; and the player turns a fragile refuge into a home worth protecting. The full saga is not yet written: Act I is built, while Acts II and III remain a mix of broad GDD direction and a detailed but unbuilt proposal in `QuestAndStoryResearch.md`.

### Cited Findings
- The old pact asks people to leave the forest room to live; the forest’s guardians keep older hungering things at bay. The Portal’s ward protects one clearing, and a tended hearth is the one thing the forest cannot forget. The player is meant to build a home so seven scattered companions can find their way back. — [GameDesignDocument.md](../../Docs/GameDesignDocument.md), §§4.1–4.5, lines 151–175.
- The opening cinematic, “The Old Story,” recounts the pact, hearth, extinguished fire, forest forgetting, Portal’s long vigil, four people who entered the woods, and four more who followed. It intentionally withholds the cause of the cold hearth, the guardians’ fate, the player’s selection, and the troll’s attack. — [BackstoryCinematic.md](../../Docs/BackstoryCinematic.md), §§1–4, especially lines 10–28 and 80–254.
- In the prologue, the player travels with Sigrid, Eirik, and Runa after Ingrid, Bjarne, Tor, and Liv, who went ahead two days earlier. The day turns to night early; Sigrid forgets Liv’s name; Sigrid’s hand does not wake the waystone, but the player’s does; the old troll comes for the stones. “The Catch” then establishes that the Portal catches only the player while seven companions scatter. — [QuestRedesign.md](../../Docs/QuestRedesign.md), §§“The story of the first act” and “The new line,” lines 106–205; [PrologueEndCinematic.md](../../Docs/PrologueEndCinematic.md), §§1–3.
- The homecoming is a second start: the Portal explains the seven are alive but being forgotten, the player lights a new hearth, frees three rootbound stones, and reopens the door to the Misty Woods. The first raid finds Ingrid and makes a successful return emotionally legible. — [GameDesignDocument.md](../../Docs/GameDesignDocument.md), §§21.1–21.4, lines 1185–1203; [QuestRedesign.md](../../Docs/QuestRedesign.md), “At home: the door” and “Raids one to four,” lines 165–185.
- Act I’s built mystery is the Heartroot: the roots that closed the road, choked the stones, and emerged from a breach’s dead are one force. The Heartroot wakes only for a Hearth Ember, is revealed as the forest’s guardian, and says “Hearth-keeper.” The Portal then admits people broke the pact first and that the guardian kept its half for ninety winters. The old troll’s road north opens. — [QuestRedesign.md](../../Docs/QuestRedesign.md), “The story of the first act,” lines 120–140; “As built: step 3,” lines 283–316.
- Rescues convert each newly felt problem into a person, home role, building, and new capability. The current rescue chain is Ingrid → Bjarne → Tor → Eirik → Yrsa → Gunnar → Liv → Asa → Runa → Hild → Sigrid; the final Sigrid rescue is the end of The Lost Expedition. — [RescueOrder.md](../../Docs/RescueOrder.md), §§“The rule” and “The order,” lines 6–48; [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), §1.5, lines 400–431.
- The GDD’s high-level saga is: Act I, find a home; Act II, gather a community and investigate the lost villages and broken pact; Act III, confront old powers and decide the relationship between settlers and spirits. — [GameDesignDocument.md](../../Docs/GameDesignDocument.md), §4.4, lines 167–174.

### Inferences
- The story’s strongest engine is not “clear increasingly difficult biomes”; it is a chain of remembered promises. The hearth, door, roots, names, and roads are one lore system that can be taught through repeated images and paid off by gameplay.
- A satisfying full campaign can grow directly from the built first act: the player has acknowledged the debt, then must learn who is waking doors, why the old troll breaks them, what the Hunger wants, and what a new pact should require.
- The rescue loop is thematically sound when each resident’s knowledge is also a human memory, not just an unlock. The current rescue order already provides a scaffold for gradually reconstructing the expedition and older wakings.

### Gaps
- The GDD describes Act II and Act III in only a few sentences; it does not establish an approved final confrontation, ending, or exact terms of a new pact. — [GameDesignDocument.md](../../Docs/GameDesignDocument.md), §4.4, lines 167–174; §26.2, line 1401.
- The built first act reaches the road north but currently has no main quest after “The Road North”; the Troll Valley opening, old troll encounter, and continued story are not implemented. — [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), §1.8, lines 559–583.

## What is approved, built, proposed, and still open

### Takeaway
Use three confidence labels in the final story proposal: built canon, user-approved but unbuilt direction, and unapproved/optional ideas. This prevents a compelling lore-bible proposal from being mistaken for content already in the game.

### Cited Findings
- **Built:** the opening and “The Catch”; the prologue’s missing-name beat and choice of why the player came; the Portal’s home dialogue; the three stones’ fragments; the first four-rescue/main-story beats; the Heartroot twist; and the title “Hearth-keeper.” `QuestRedesign.md` states the first three redesign steps are built, though not played through by hand at the time it was written. — [QuestRedesign.md](../../Docs/QuestRedesign.md), status at lines 1–6 and “As built: step 2–3,” lines 257–316; [GameDesignDocument.md](../../Docs/GameDesignDocument.md), §21.0, lines 1110–1183.
- **User-approved direction, unbuilt as story content (2026-11-15):** the antagonist is the Hunger with an unnamed man in grey/Waker as its human face; he wakes doors to recover his taken family, and each cold door lets the Hunger further in. The player wakes the stones because “You kept their fire,” not through bloodline. Four additional residents were taken in earlier wakings, with time slipping in the mist. The old troll is a guardian who smashes doors and can be fought or paid. Sigrid’s past and an Act II ending at her rescue in Troll Valley are approved directions; Eirik’s sister Sigrun is approved as a twelfth rescue. — [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), “Status and the user’s decisions,” lines 19–38.
- The proposed Act II, “The Doors,” would follow the Troll’s Road, reveal broken waystones and the Waker’s camp, culminate in a fight-or-toll choice with the old troll, then rescue Sigrid and answer “why me.” This quest sequence is a proposal, not built story content. — [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), §3.7.2, lines 1389–1405.
- The proposed Act III goes to the Frost Peaks/First Hearth, confronts the Waker and Hunger, includes a marsh guardian, and lets the player set terms for a new pact. It is explicitly an outline to agree before writing. — [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), §3.7.3, lines 1407–1412; [GameDesignDocument.md](../../Docs/GameDesignDocument.md), §4.4, lines 167–174.
- The claim that the Portal is the first hearth-keeper transformed into a *gardvord*, and that the pact has three keepers (people/hearth, forest/Heartroot, mountain/old troll), is labeled a proposed reveal/addition, not a confirmed decision. Treat it as an optional story choice, not established lore. — [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), §§3.8.1 and 3.8.4, lines 1430–1460.
- An important document conflict remains: GDD §15.3 lists an older rescue order (including Yrsa after Asa and Liv after Runa), while the later `RescueOrder.md` and the data order put Yrsa before Gunnar, Liv before Asa, and Sigrid last. The 2026-11-15 research document itself flags GDD §15.3 as stale. — [GameDesignDocument.md](../../Docs/GameDesignDocument.md), §15.3, lines 883–890; [RescueOrder.md](../../Docs/RescueOrder.md), lines 26–48; [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), §1.8, lines 593–606.
- The exact ending type is open: alternate authored endings versus a mostly linear resolution with local choices has not been decided. — [GameDesignDocument.md](../../Docs/GameDesignDocument.md), §26.2, line 1401.

### Inferences
- The approved Waker motive gives the Hunger a human conduit and a tragic pressure: the antagonist wants a family back, but every attempt worsens the condition that erased them. That can support negotiation without excusing the harm.
- “You kept their fire” ties the player’s ordinary prologue action to the identity “Hearth-keeper.” It is a more coherent answer than bloodline destiny because the story makes care, not ancestry, the source of agency.
- The old troll’s role as door-breaker/guardian can turn the apparent monster into an obstacle with a defensible motive. The fight-or-toll ending should reveal that motive before the player commits.
- The story proposal should keep the Portal’s gardvord origin and the third-keeper cosmology out of the canon column until explicitly selected; they can deepen the world, but they alter the identity of the narrator and the pact’s rules.

### Gaps
- The accepted decisions have not yet been embodied in a complete quest/dialogue chain, so the exact speakers, timing, scene evidence, and wording for the new reveals remain to be authored.
- The Old Troll/Waker/Hunger triangle needs a causality map: who opens which doors, what the troll can sense, why it smashes the Haven portal, and what happens if the player pays the toll instead of fighting.
- The final pact needs explicit stakes and limits: who gives what up, what the Hunger is permitted to do, what the forest guardians regain, and how the choice changes gameplay and the epilogue.

## What a complete story must pay off and reconcile

### Takeaway
The narrative needs a payoff for every deliberately planted question and every accepted direction, while preserving the first-act reveal. The strongest full arc should make the player’s homebuilding, rescues, extraction decisions, and eventual pact choice all versions of the same act: remembering and bringing people home.

### Cited Findings
- The first 15 minutes deliberately plant three questions: where the sun went; what the forest is doing (Sigrid blanks on Liv’s name); and why the stones wake for the player. The first Act pays the first two through the Heartroot/forgetting lore and leaves “why me” for later. — [QuestRedesign.md](../../Docs/QuestRedesign.md), “The story of the first act,” lines 111–136.
- The player’s prologue choice is coin, kin, or truth; the Portal’s end-of-Act-I conversation is intended to answer each motive differently. — [QuestRedesign.md](../../Docs/QuestRedesign.md), lines 138–140; [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), §1.7, lines 475–513.
- Four current missing expedition members plus three starter companions account for the Portal’s seven scattered sparks; four strangers (Yrsa, Gunnar, Asa, Hild) are proposed as survivors of earlier wakings and explain why eleven recruits can eventually come home. Time slippage in the mist is the chosen explanation. — [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), user decisions lines 30–33; §1.7, lines 520–553; §3.8.3, lines 1446–1450.
- The current GDD rescue model emphasizes one person per rescue, recruited through find/contact/secure/escort/extract/welcome/job, and says a person not extracted does not magically arrive at Haven. — [GameDesignDocument.md](../../Docs/GameDesignDocument.md), §§15.1–15.3, lines 864–890; [RescueOrder.md](../../Docs/RescueOrder.md), §§“The rule” and “The problem comes first,” lines 6–65.
- Lore seeds explicitly identified as unanswered include the Waker opening doors, what the Hunger is, the old troll’s reason, the mail-clad forgotten person, Eirik’s sister, Sigrid’s knowledge, and the old strangers’ relation to the “seven.” — [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), §1.8, lines 559–593.
- The proposed Act II puts Sigrid’s rescue in Troll Valley, followed by “What Sigrid Knows”; the proposed Act III returns to the First Hearth in Frost Peaks and resolves the Waker/Hunger and new pact. — [QuestAndStoryResearch.md](../../Docs/QuestAndStoryResearch.md), §§3.7.2–3.7.3, lines 1389–1412.

### Inferences
- A useful act structure is: **Act I: the debt** (the people broke the hearth pact and the Heartroot paid); **Act II: the doors** (prove someone is deliberately waking stones, let the troll’s guardian motive complicate the hunt, rescue Sigrid and learn the player was chosen by care); **Act III: the first hearth** (face the Waker, the Hunger, and the original terms, then choose a new reciprocal pact).
- Repeated physical clues can make the lore legible without exposition: hearth flame lights a rune; the same root pattern appears on roads, stones, and creatures; lost names are cut into wood/stone; door rings point toward the First Hearth; troll damage is directed at the doorway rather than people.
- The Waker’s family should not simply be a final boss motivation that excuses him. The climax can ask whether to close the doors (protecting Haven but leaving the lost), restore controlled doors (risk with clear consent), or form a new pact with guardians and the Hunger’s captive dead—provided each ending is mechanically distinct and no route casually cures all consequences.
- The player’s motive choice (coin/kin/truth) should affect dialogue and local consequences, while the final pact choice determines the broad world state. This respects the GDD’s preference for local choices over a branching cinematic maze.
- Avoid changing survivor count or sequence casually: seven current sparks, four earlier-taken strangers, eleven listed recruits, and a proposed twelfth (Sigrun) can be coherent, but the script must state which group “seven” refers to and when the Portal first heard each group.

### Gaps
- The exact roles and identities of the four earlier-taken strangers are only partly described. Their memories and how they relate to the Waker/Hunger are not established in built dialogue.
- The planned ending has no defined outcomes, implementation constraints, or test criteria. A complete proposal should make the choices and epilogues explicit while marking them as new recommendations.
- A story bible should reconcile the date/interval of the Hearth going cold, the Heartroot’s ninety winters of resistance, the Portal’s hundred-winter vigil, and the recent acceleration (proposed as the Waker’s door-waking over the last year).
- The latest research doc contains multiple “Status and the user’s decisions,” “Built,” “proposed,” and “Open questions” labels. Those labels should be preserved; a decision that a direction is wanted does not mean the quests/dialogue or all detailed lore are implemented.
